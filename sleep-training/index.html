Hallwatch: Sleep Training

A night log for baby check-ins

Seven nights of check-in waits, drawn as one ladder: tonight's wait fills the gap between two rungs, the Lock Screen tells you when to go in, and each night is written under the one before. That is the whole idea, for parents in the first hard week of sleep training who keep losing count in a dark hallway.

TONIGHT'S LADDER
Progressive waiting starts at 3, 5 and 10 minutes on night 1 and grows a little each night, up to 20, 25 and 30 on night 7. Tap Lights out and the first wait fills the gap between two rungs. When it's full, go in, tap Went in, keep it short, and tap Back out as you close the door: the next wait starts then. A waking at 2 am starts again at that night's first wait.

ON THE LOCK SCREEN
Leave the phone face up by the door. The Lock Screen and the Dynamic Island show the wait you're on and "Go in at 7:53", counting down by the clock even when the phone is locked, with Went in, Back out and Asleep buttons so you never unlock it in the dark. An alert sounds when it's time to go in.

THE WEEK, NIGHT UNDER NIGHT
Every night is written onto its own small ladder with an asleep mark where it ended and the minutes to sleep underneath. "Night 4: asleep in 18 min, before the second check. Night 3 took three checks." Tap a night to see its check-in times and correct one you tapped late.

FREE, ALL OF IT
The whole progressive-waiting program is free, every night, with the Lock Screen countdown. Nobody pays on night 2 of the hardest week.

HALLWATCH PRO, ONE PURCHASE
- Timed checks in two-minute steps, or the waits your pediatrician or consultant gave you
- The week's ladder as a picture for the other parent, and a CSV of your nights
- Keep each program: a second child, or a fresh start after a cold

WHERE THE WAITS COME FROM
The waits are the published progressive-waiting chart and, for timed checks, the two-minute steps from published infant-sleep studies, with the sources inside the app. The safe-sleep page quotes the American Academy of Pediatrics' 2022 recommendations. Hallwatch is published guidance, not medical advice: talk to your pediatrician before you start. It doesn't listen to your baby, track feeds or naps, or tell you whether to sleep train.

YOUR NIGHTS STAY ON THIS IPHONE
No account, no iCloud, no analytics. The week leaves the phone only as a picture you choose to send.
