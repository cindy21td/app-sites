Tank Tag for Betta Fish Care

Chart the Change in a Fin

Kai's caudal split on November 2. At feeding time his keeper leaned into the tank, opened his tag and tapped the tail on the drawing of him until it read "splits or holes". The edge redrew torn, and the tag said so in a sentence.

Two days later the camera showed the last photo of Kai faintly over the tank, so the new one lined up with it. The tail was rough at the edge but whole. One tap stepped it back to "ragged edge". The earlier split stayed dashed over the drawing, and the tag read "Since Nov 2: caudal better."

The water went on the same tank: 25% every Sunday, counted in days on the tag and on the Lock Screen. Ammonia, nitrite and nitrate went in as the kit read them, with no grades.

By December 14 the fin was full again. Six weekly photos, all lined up, played back as a short film with the date and the grade under each frame: 42 days from splits to full.

That is Tank Tag: a tank tag for each fish, drawn from the card a breeder clips to the tank. Each fin is graded on the outline of its own tail type, so a plakat's short fins and a female's are full at their own length. A divided tank keeps one water log for every section. A new tank's readings are drawn as the stages of cycling, with the day each one began. When a fish looks off, a dated course holds what you see and what you do, and a one-page vet sheet takes the whole record to a fish vet. All of that is free for up to two fish.

Premium adds the regrowth film and the colour record, which keeps a marble betta's colours from each photo on a dated strip. It also adds more than one photo at a check-in, any two dates side by side, the tank tag widget, reminders, an iCloud backup that includes the photos, and more than two fish.

Tank Tag doesn't diagnose illness or recommend medicines or doses. For a sick fish, see a fish vet and bring the vet sheet.

Nothing leaves the phone except the iCloud backup, if you turn it on: no usage tracking, no crash-reporting service, no account to create.
