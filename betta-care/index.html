Betta Fish Keeper: Care Log

Fin Rot, Tank & Water Change

Grade his fins, note how he's acting, and see what changed since the last time you looked. Betta Keeper gives each betta his own tank tag: his photo, his name, a fin chart and his water-change routine, on the first screen you see.

Tap a fin on his outline to grade it, from full to ragged edge, splits or holes, lost length, or to the body. Pick what he's doing: flaring, building a bubble nest, clamped, resting on the bottom, spitting food. The tag then tells you "Since Nov 2: caudal better" in plain words. Check-ins, water changes and illness courses are free, with backdating and no paywall in the way while he's unwell.

FREE
• A tank tag for each fish: name, tail type (veiltail, halfmoon, crowntail, plakat, delta, rosetail, double tail), colour, days home and one cover photo
• Check-ins with a fin chart for dorsal, caudal, anal, pelvic and pectoral fins, behaviour chips, AM and PM feeding ticks, the food he ate and the water temperature
• A water-change log per tank: percent or gallons with the other worked out as you type, gravel vac, conditioner, new water temperature, and how many days since the last change
• A course for when he looks off: what you see, what you're doing in your own words, a tick for each day, and how it ended
• Optional water test numbers (ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, pH) on the tank page, shown as your kit reads them
• Up to 2 fish. No account and no ads

PREMIUM
• A photo at every check-in, a photo timeline, and any two dates side by side with both fin charts, so you can see his fins grow back
• Reminders for water changes, feeding times and each step of a course
• A one-page vet sheet PDF with his routine, the last 30 days of check-ins and every course, plus CSV export
• iCloud backup of your records and unlimited fish

Betta Keeper is a fish journal for betta keepers, whether he came home from the shop in a cup or from a breeder, and for anyone keeping a Siamese fighting fish in a 2.5 to 10 gallon tank. It is a record of what you see and do. It does not diagnose fin rot or any illness, and it never suggests a medicine or a dose. For a sick fish, see a fish vet and bring the vet sheet.
