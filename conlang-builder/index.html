Daughter Tongue: Conlang Lab

IPA chart, words, sound laws

Daughter Tongue is a workbench for conlangers. It opens on the IPA pulmonic chart, the one from every phonetics handout, and you fill it in by tapping the sounds your speakers use. The count above it tells you where your inventory sits: "17 consonants. Moderately small: 21.7% of 563 surveyed languages (WALS)."

Then it does what a spreadsheet can't. Write a sound law in the notation you already use, t → d / V_V, and every word in your lexicon shows its new form beside the old one, the changed sounds underlined. Order the laws, turn one off, peek at the words before any of them ran. When the result looks right, grow it into a daughter language: every word keeps its ancestor, and the family tree shows how your languages are related.

THE CHART
• All 59 pulmonic consonants and 28 vowels, arranged as the chart prints them, with impossible articulations hatched
• Tap a manner row to open it into large keys; on a wide screen, tap the cells directly
• Affricates, clicks, ejectives and tones as your own extra sounds
• A spelling for every sound, so words are written your way
• Vowel count and syllable type against the WALS survey

WORDS
• A word forge: a syllable pattern such as (C)(L)V(C) over your sound classes, banned sequences, one to four syllables; keep the candidates you like
• A lexicon with spelling, IPA, part of speech, meaning and notes, searchable by sound or by meaning
• CSV in and out, so the spreadsheet you already have comes with you
• One page of grammar notes per language

SOUND LAWS AND DAUGHTERS
• Ordered laws built from your sounds and classes, previewed live on every word
• A trace for each word: the form after each law, one line at a time
• Daughter languages that remember each word's ancestor, and a family tree
• A typeset reference PDF: chart, laws, notes and dictionary

No account, no server, no feed. Your languages stay on your iPhone, and the sample family (Latin to Western Romance, labelled as a sample) shows how it works before you start your own.
