Pour Plan for Soap Layers

Split your batter by color

Three colors in one loaf means three pitchers on the bench, and each one needs its own share of the batter. Draw the bar you want to cut and the pour is worked out from the drawing: the grams of batter for every pitcher, the colorant that goes into it, and the order to pour.

See the bars before you pour
Set the bar thickness and the cut preview lays out every bar's face along the loaf. Layers look the same in each bar; swirls drift from one bar to the next. It is a preview of a hand-poured loaf, and your bars will differ.

Pour from the plan at the bench
Start pouring and the plan goes one pitcher per screen, in big type for gloved hands: the grams, the colorant and how to disperse it, what to do with the pitcher, and which one comes next. The screen stays awake, and a timer counts only when you start it.

A three-layer loaf, start to finish
Tap Layers and an empty loaf mold fills with three bands. Drag a line and the grams in both pitchers change with it. Tap a band and pick a colorant: the drawing recolors, and the step says how much to use and how to disperse it.

Sized to your mold
Enter your mold's inside size, loaf, slab, round or cavity tray, and the fill height, and the batch is sized to fill it, using the soap makers' rule of thumb of 0.40 oz of oils per cubic inch. For an exact number, weigh the water that fills your mold.

What is free and what is Pro
Free: layered bars of up to three layers with mica lines, the cut preview, the pour session, the built-in colorants and oils, and all of the lye math. Pour Plan Pro, bought once, adds in-the-pot, drop and hanger swirls, column pour, tiger stripe and gradient layers with up to six portions, a fourth layer, saved designs with their recipes as files you can share, a shelf of your molds, the pour card to print, your own colorants and oils, fragrance and additives, swatches photographed from your cured bars, and cost per bar from your own prices.

Colorants it knows
19 pigments, oxides, clays, micas and plant colorants, each with a usage rate you can change and a note on how it behaves in lye, scaled to the oils in each portion. The drawing shows the planned look; plant colorants and micas are flagged because some shift in lye.

The lye math behind the drawing
NaOH, KOH or both, with superfat, lye purity and three water settings. 23 oils come with saponification (SAP) ranges and the published source for each. A worst-case superfat check tells you when a blend could run lye-heavy. You also get the blend's fatty-acid profile and iodine value, and a weigh-out list.

Lye is caustic. Wear gloves and goggles, add lye to water, never water to lye, and check any recipe before you pour. Pour Plan calculates lye, water and colorant amounts; fragrance limits and cosmetic labeling rules are yours to check.
