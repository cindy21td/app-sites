Dim Sum Teahouse Dumpling Game

Lift steamers, serve each cart

Help Bao, the apprentice server, sort the bamboo steamers at a busy teahouse. Every cart serves certain dishes: har gow, siu mai, char siu bao and lo mai gai. Lift steamers onto your stack and set them down where they belong.

The catch: you can only lift the steamer nearest the aisle, and your stack is last on, first off. Bury the wrong basket and you'll have to dig it out again. Each table has a target, the fewest moves it can be served in, and Auntie Mei knows when you have matched it.

WATCH THE PERFECT SERVICE
When a table is served, play the fewest-moves service beside your own, move by move. Each detour you took is marked with the moves it cost, and you can serve the table again from just before it.

THE MENU BOOK
A page opens for each dish the first time you serve it: what it is, what is inside, how it is eaten, and its name in Chinese characters with Jyutping. Yum cha and the bamboo steamer have pages of their own.

SET YOUR OWN TABLE
Choose the carts, place the steamers and decide which cart serves what. Your iPhone checks that the table can be served and finds its target before anyone plays it. Send it to a friend as a short code; it opens with your name on it.

ONE DAY OF SERVICE
Five sittings run from morning yum cha to the lantern supper, with the light in the moon window changing as the day goes on. Longer carts, paired tickets that finish side by side and one-steamer hands arrive through the day. Steamers differ by shape as well as colour, undo and starting over are free, and the teahouse tune can be turned off. It all plays offline, and your progress stays on your iPhone.

HOW THE MENU IS PRICED
Morning yum cha, 42 tables, is on the house, and so are the menu book and the table editor. The brunch rush, lunch service, afternoon tea and the lantern supper, 158 tables, come together as the full menu: bought once, shared with your family, never a subscription.
