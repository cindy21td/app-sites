Bitcue Chiptune Game Loops

Calm and battle 8-bit music

One loop for one level of your game, in three intensities. It is already playing when you open the song: Calm. Push the lever and the drums come in on the next bar line (Alert). Push it again and the lead enters (Battle). All three are the same loop, bar for bar, so your game can switch the moment the player is spotted.

Start from a level, not a blank grid: Village, Forest, Cave, Desert, Snowfield, Castle, Ruins or Starfield. You get eight bars on four chiptune voices, two pulse waves, a triangle bass and noise drums, written from the level's scale, chord progression and tempo. Roll for another take, or change the key, scale and tempo.

Then make it yours in the piano roll. Every note belongs to the intensity it enters on: outlined notes are Calm, hatched notes Alert, solid notes Battle. The calm version never loses what you wrote, and the battle version always includes it. Shape each voice's pulse width, decay and volume.

Export a loop as a WAV that is exact to the sample, with loop points in the file and the last notes folded back into the start, so it repeats without a click.

Bitcue Pro adds:
- The matched set: calm, alert and battle loops of identical length, three stems that add up to Battle, and a cue sheet (tempo, bars, loop length in samples) for your engine's interactive music. In Godot the stems go straight into a synchronized stream.
- MIDI export, a track per voice and intensity, for your DAW or retro composer
- 16 and 32 bar loops

Songs are .bitcue files in Files (Finder on a Mac), so they can live in your game's project folder. Works on iPhone, iPad and Mac.
