Koi Pond Almanac

Feed bands by water temp

Every morning you lift the pond thermometer. Koi Pond Almanac puts that reading on your pond's year, says which feed band the water is in, how many days it has been there, and how many grams your fish get today.

THE WATER YEAR
Your morning temperatures drawn across the year over the feed bands of the SRAC 435 feeding table: below 45 °F, 45–49, 50–59, 60–69, 70–90 and above 90. When the water settles into a new band (two readings in a row, at least 12 hours apart), the almanac marks the date. One warm afternoon in March is a touch, not a crossing. Press and hold the year to read any morning.

READ THE WATER
Drag the bead up the drawn thermometer, or type the number. The band, its words and the grams move with it. Add a kit reading when you test: pH, total ammonia, nitrite, KH, GH and dissolved oxygen, each marked ok, watch or act with its source, and ammonia shown as its toxic share at that pH and temperature. Or say "Log pond temperature" to Siri with wet hands.

COUNT THE FISH, NOT PROFILES
No page per koi. Count them the way you sort them in the net: how many at 8 inches, at 14, at 20. Weights come from the carp length table in SRAC 435, and the census gives today's ration and the stocking load for your pond's gallons.

THE MONTHS
The almanac's months sit beside the year: each with its trace, its crossings, its readings and tests. Your records are one plain file per year on your phone, visible in the Files app.

PRO
Last year's line under this year's, with the crossing dates side by side ("50 °F: 3 days earlier than last year"), earlier years kept readable, and salt and nitrite doses worked out for your pond's volume. Seven days free, then $19.99 a year or $2.99 a month.

Feed bands, marks and doses come from US extension publications (SRAC, UF/IFAS, Texas A&M AgriLife, US EPA). They are guidelines for ponds. The app doesn't diagnose sick fish (see a fish vet for that), and none of these bodies is connected with the app.
