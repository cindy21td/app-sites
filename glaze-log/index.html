Glaze Test Tiles for Pottery

Each layer through the kiln

What glaze was that, and what went over what?

A test in Glaze Log starts at the jar shelf. Tap the jars in the order they go on, with the coats and whether each was dipped, brushed, poured or sprayed, and the app draws the stack as a cross-section of the tile: Rutile, 1 coat, over Celadon, 2 coats, on porcelain at cone 6. It gives the test a four-character code to pencil on the back of the tile, from letters and numbers that are hard to misread in handwriting.

Then the tile goes in the kiln, and the firing gets its own sheet: the schedule as you fired it, segment by segment with rate, target and hold; the cone you aimed at; what the witness cones did on each shelf; and a map of the kiln where you drag each tile to the place it sat. Glaze Log records a firing. It does not control or time a kiln.

When the kiln opens, Unload hands you the waiting tests one at a time. Type the code from the back of a tile to jump to it, take a photo, and mark it Keep, Adjust or Never. If something went wrong, touch the photo where it happened: crawling, pinholes, crazing, shivering, running or blistering. A mark records what you saw. The app does not say why it happened.

After that the tile is on the Board with every other fired result. Filter it by glaze, clay, cone, firing or fault, and open any result to read it against its firing and its place in the kiln.

All of that is free, with no limit on tests or firings, and so are the copy of your records in your own iCloud and the full export.

Glaze Log Pro is for reading across many results at once. Over/Under takes two glazes and shows both orders on every clay body, with the pairs you have never fired marked. Every firing lines up one glaze's results side by side by cone, by clay or by kiln. Blend boards lay out a triaxial blend of three glazes or a two-way grid, give the amount to mix for each tile, make a coded test for every tile, and after the firing show the board as a picture built from your photos. The recipe bench keeps a base and its additions, saved versions, batch sheets in g, oz or lb, and line blends. Test sheets print as a PDF for the studio wall.

Who it is for: potters who test glazes in small batches, in a community studio, a class or at home. It does not follow a piece through throwing, trimming and bisque, it is not glaze chemistry software, and it says nothing about food safety or materials.

There is no account and the app collects no data. Your records stay on your phone and in your own iCloud.

Glaze Log Pro costs $3.99 a month or $24.99 a year, and each plan begins with 14 days free, long enough for a test to be glazed, fired and unloaded. It is an auto-renewing subscription: after the free two weeks your Apple ID is charged, and again each period until you cancel, which you can do up to 24 hours before a period ends under Settings › Apple ID › Subscriptions. If you stop, everything you recorded stays readable and exportable; Over/Under, Every firing, new blend boards, new recipes and new PDFs are what lock.
